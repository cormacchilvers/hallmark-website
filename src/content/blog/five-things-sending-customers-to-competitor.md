Most business owners know their website isn't quite right. They just don't know which part is costing them the most.

The honest answer: it's usually not one thing. It's five things, all pulling in the same direction. Here's what to look for.

## 1. Your phone number isn't visible on every page

This sounds so obvious that people assume they've done it. Most haven't.

A lot of business websites have a phone number in the footer. Or on the contact page. Or somewhere on the homepage, if you scroll down far enough.

That's not enough.

Your phone number should be at the very top of every page, on every device, and tappable on mobile. The moment a visitor can't immediately find your number, most of them are gone. They'll search your competitor and call them instead.

Test yours right now. Open your website on your phone. Without scrolling, can you tap a number and call? If the answer is no, or if there's any hesitation, you're losing calls every week.

## 2. The site takes more than three seconds to load

Half the people who visit your website will leave if it doesn't load within three seconds. That's not an estimate, it's a pattern that's been studied to death by Google and every major analytics company.

Slow websites are caused by a short list of problems: oversized images straight from a camera roll, bloated page builders, or cheap shared hosting. You probably won't notice the slowness on a fast WiFi connection at home. Your customer notices on 4G signal in a car park.

You can test your own site right now. Search "PageSpeed Insights" on Google. Enter your URL. Anything below 50 on mobile is actively pushing people away.

## 3. It's not clear what you do in the first five seconds

This is the most common problem on small business websites, and it comes from the same source every time: the business owner knows what they do, so they forget to actually say it.

"Welcome to ABC Plumbing, your trusted local plumber" tells someone almost nothing. Welcome where? Trusted by whom? Do you cover my area?

"Emergency plumber covering Chelmsford, Colchester, and Brentwood, call now for a same-day quote" is a completely different thing. It tells the visitor what you do, where you do it, who you're for, and what happens next.

When someone lands on your homepage, they should know within five seconds: who you are, what you do, where you do it, and how to reach you. If it takes longer than that, many of them won't wait.

## 4. There's no clear next step

You've got someone on your website. They're interested. Now what?

If your answer is "they'll find the contact page", you're losing people.

Every page should have one obvious action, usually a button that says something specific. Not "click here." Not "contact us." Something like "Call for a same-day quote" or "Book a free consultation."

That button should stand out, a different colour from everything else on the page. It should be big enough to tap easily on a phone. And it should appear more than once on a long page, so the visitor doesn't have to scroll all the way back up to take action.

## 5. Your Google Business Profile is empty or missing

This isn't technically "on your website," but it's often the first thing a potential customer sees before they even reach your site.

Your Google Business Profile is the listing that appears on Google Maps and in the local search results. If it's incomplete, no photos, outdated address, no description of what you do, Google deprioritises you. Customers click on the listings that look established and active.

If you've never claimed your Google Business Profile, you may not even appear at all. Someone in your area could be searching for your exact service right now and not finding you.

## The fix

None of these are major projects. Most can be addressed in a few hours:

- Move your phone number to the header of every page
- Compress your images and test your page speed
- Rewrite your homepage headline so it says what you do, where, and why to call
- Add a prominent, clearly coloured button to every page
- Claim and complete your Google Business Profile
